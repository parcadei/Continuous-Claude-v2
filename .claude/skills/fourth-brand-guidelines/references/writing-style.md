# Fourth Writing Style Guide

## Grammar and Style

**Active Voice (always):**
- DO: "Fourth iQ analyzes patterns"
- DON'T: "Patterns are analyzed by Fourth iQ"

**Present tense for features:**
- DO: "Fourth iQ identifies opportunities"
- DON'T: "Fourth iQ will identify opportunities"

**Second person for customers:**
- DO: "Your team gains visibility"
- DON'T: "Teams gain visibility"

**Sentence length:** Target 15–20 words. Max 25. Vary length for rhythm.

**Plain language:**
- "use" NOT "utilize"
- "help" NOT "facilitate"
- "change" NOT "transform" (unless major transformation)
- Avoid: leverage, synergy, paradigm, disrupt

## Punctuation

- **Oxford comma:** Always use
- **Exclamation points:** Max one per page; only for genuine excitement; never in formal communications
- **Em dash (—):** Emphasis or breaks
- **En dash (–):** Ranges
- **Colons:** Introduce lists or explanations
- **Semicolons:** Connect related independent clauses

## Capitalization

**Title Case** (headlines, titles, proper nouns, product names)

**Sentence Case** (body text, bullet points when complete sentences, subheadings optional)

**Special cases:**
- Fourth — always capitalized
- iQ — always lowercase i, uppercase Q
- Fourth Dimension — both words capitalized

## Spelling Standards

American English: -ize (not -ise), -or (not -our), -er (not -re)

**Consistent terms:**
- workforce (one word), email (one word)
- login (noun) / log in (verb)
- setup (noun) / set up (verb)

## Content Structure

**Standard flow:** Hook → Problem → Solution → Evidence → Action

**Headlines:**
- Front-load key information, active verbs, specific (numbers/outcomes)
- Under 60 characters for web
- DO: "Reduce Labor Costs by 15% with Fourth iQ"
- DON'T: "Fourth iQ: Workforce Management Solution"

**Body content:**
- Short paragraphs (3–4 sentences max)
- Subheadings every 2–3 paragraphs
- Max 12 words per line for clarity

**Bullet points:**
- Parallel construction (same part of speech to start each)
- Consistent punctuation, one line preferred (two max)
- Action-oriented

**CTAs — action verbs, specific outcome, 2–5 words:**
- "Request a demo"
- "See Fourth iQ in action"
- "Download case study"
- "Schedule consultation"
- "Get the guide"
- NOT: "Click here" or "Learn more"
