## 1. Headline Formulas

Target: 6-12 words. Flesch-Kincaid grade 6-8. Benefit-focused, specific, passes the 5-second test.

### Primary Formulas (Highest Converting)

**[Outcome] without [Pain]**
- "Beautiful schedules without the spreadsheet chaos"
- "Faster hiring without the manual sorting"
- "Full compliance without the paperwork"

**[Outcome] in [Timeframe]**
- "Cut scheduling time in half this week"
- "Launch your menu in 5 minutes"
- "Onboard new hires in one day, not one week"

**The [better way] to [task]**
- "The faster way to build restaurant schedules"
- "The smarter way to forecast labor costs"
- "A better way to manage your team"

**Stop [pain]. Start [outcome].**
- "Stop guessing staffing levels. Start knowing."
- "Stop losing candidates to slow hiring. Start winning them."
- "Stop drowning in spreadsheets. Start leading."

**[Number] [things] to [outcome]**
- "3 steps to 40% lower labor costs"
- "One platform for every location"
- "5 minutes to your perfect schedule"

### Secondary Formulas

**[Specific result] for [specific audience]**
- "80% faster scheduling for multi-unit restaurants"
- "Real-time labor insights for operations directors"

**What if [desirable scenario]?**
- "What if your schedule built itself?"
- "What if you never overstaffed again?"

**[Audience], meet [solution].**
- "Restaurant managers, meet your AI scheduler."
- "HR teams, meet frictionless onboarding."

### The 4U Test

Score every headline against Masterson's 4U Framework:
- **Useful**: Does it promise a clear benefit?
- **Urgent**: Is there a reason to act now?
- **Unique**: Does it differentiate from competitors?
- **Ultra-Specific**: Does it include numbers, timeframes, or measurable claims?

A headline scoring 3/4 or 4/4 is ready. Below 3/4, rewrite.

## 2. Subheadline Patterns

Target: 15-25 words. Extends (never repeats) the headline. Explains "how" or adds specificity.

**"How" extension:**
- Headline: "Cut scheduling time by 80%"
- Sub: "AI-powered scheduling builds your perfect roster in minutes, not hours — across every location."

**Proof extension:**
- Headline: "The smarter way to manage your team"
- Sub: "Trusted by 500+ restaurant groups to reduce labor costs by an average of 12%."

**Audience + value extension:**
- Headline: "One platform for every location"
- Sub: "Give GMs, ops directors, and corporate teams a single source of truth for scheduling, labor, and compliance."

**Rule**: If the headline is creative or clever, the subheadline must be crystal clear. If the headline is direct, the subheadline adds proof or specificity.

## 3. CTA Copy Templates

### Primary CTA (Hero + Final Section)

Pattern: `[Action Verb] + [Specific Value]`

**High performers:**
- "Start My Free Trial"
- "Get My Free Schedule Demo"
- "See It In Action — Free"
- "Build My First Schedule"
- "Request My Custom Demo"
- "Try Free for 14 Days"

**With urgency layer (Final CTA):**
- "Claim Your Free Trial — Limited Spots"
- "Start Free Today — No Card Required"
- "Get Started Now — Setup Takes 5 Minutes"

### Secondary CTA (subordinate, lower commitment)

- "See How It Works" (links to How It Works section or video)
- "View Pricing"
- "Read Case Studies"
- "Talk to Sales"

### CTA Anti-Patterns (avoid these)

- "Submit" — generic, zero value communicated
- "Learn More" — passive, doesn't promise anything
- "Click Here" — meaningless
- "Sign Up" — implies commitment without showing value
- "Register" — bureaucratic, cold

### First-Person vs. Second-Person

First-person pronouns ("my", "me") outperform second-person ("your", "you") by +90% CTR:
- "Start **My** Free Trial" beats "Start **Your** Free Trial"
- "Get **My** Custom Report" beats "Get **A** Custom Report"

## 4. Microcopy Library

Place directly below or beside the CTA button. Reduces friction and anxiety.

### Risk Reducers (adjacent to CTA)
- "No credit card required"
- "Free forever — no hidden fees"
- "Cancel anytime, no questions asked"
- "Setup takes 2 minutes"
- "No contract. No commitment."
- "Your data stays private — we never share or sell it."
- "30-day money-back guarantee"
- "Includes free onboarding support"

### Form Reassurance (below form fields)
- "We'll never share your email. Privacy policy."
- "Your information is encrypted and secure."
- "We respect your inbox — expect 1-2 emails per week."
- "Unsubscribe with one click, anytime."

### Social Proof Microcopy (near CTA)
- "Join 10,000+ restaurant professionals"
- "Rated 4.8/5 by 500+ customers"
- "Used by [Brand], [Brand], and [Brand]"
- "Featured in [Publication]"

### Urgency Microcopy (use sparingly and honestly)
- "Limited to first 100 signups"
- "Offer ends [date]"
- "Only 3 demo slots left this week"
- "Free tier closes March 31"

## 5. Section Copy Patterns

### Problem Section

**Goal**: Make the visitor feel understood. Name their pain specifically.

**Pattern**: 2-3 short paragraphs. First-person or second-person voice. Specific details, not generalities.

**Template**:
```
You know the drill. Every [time period], you [painful task]. It takes [specific time/effort],
and by the time you're done, [consequence of the pain].

Meanwhile, [additional frustration]. Your [stakeholders] are asking for [thing you can't deliver],
and your current tools weren't built for [what you actually need].

There's a better way.
```

**Example**:
```
You know the drill. Every Sunday night, you're hunched over a spreadsheet
building next week's schedule. It takes 4-6 hours, and by Monday morning,
three people have already called in sick.

Meanwhile, your labor costs keep creeping up because you're guessing
at demand instead of forecasting it. Your district manager wants a
report you can't pull without exporting five different files.

There's a better way.
```

### Features Section

**Pattern per feature**: Benefit headline + 1-2 sentence description using FAB model.

**Template**:
```
### [Benefit-Focused Headline]
[Feature name] [what it does] so you can [benefit to the user].
[One sentence with specific proof point or differentiator].
```

**Example**:
```
### Build Schedules in Minutes, Not Hours
AI auto-scheduling analyzes demand patterns and employee availability
to generate your optimal roster in under 5 minutes.
Managers report saving 6+ hours per week on average.
```

### How It Works Section

**Pattern**: 3 numbered steps. Simple, achievable, builds confidence.

**Template**:
```
1. [Low-friction start] — [One sentence. Emphasize ease.]
2. [Core value delivery] — [One sentence. Show the magic.]
3. [Outcome achieved] — [One sentence. Paint the result.]
```

**Example**:
```
1. Connect your POS — One-click integration with Toast, Square, and 50+ systems.
2. Watch AI build your schedule — Our engine factors in demand, availability, and labor targets.
3. Publish and relax — Your team gets notified instantly. Shift swaps happen automatically.
```

### FAQ Section

**Pattern**: Question as the visitor would ask it (not formal). Answer leads with the reassurance.

**Template**:
```
Q: [Objection phrased as a question]
A: [Direct reassurance first]. [Then explain why/how].
```

**Example**:
```
Q: What if my team isn't tech-savvy?
A: Most managers are up and running in under an hour. The interface is designed
for busy restaurant professionals, not IT teams. Plus, every plan includes
free onboarding support.
```

## 6. Persuasion Triggers

Weave these into copy naturally. Based on Cialdini's 7 Principles.

| Principle | How to Apply | Example Copy |
|-----------|-------------|--------------|
| **Reciprocity** | Give value before asking | "Download our free labor cost calculator" |
| **Scarcity** | Limited availability | "First 50 signups get priority onboarding" |
| **Social Proof** | Show others trust you | "500+ restaurant groups trust us daily" |
| **Authority** | Expert endorsement | "Recommended by the National Restaurant Association" |
| **Commitment** | Small yes before big yes | "Take the 2-minute scheduling quiz" |
| **Liking** | Mirror the audience | "Built by restaurant people, for restaurant people" |
| **Unity** | Shared identity | "Join the community of operators who refuse to settle" |

## 7. Words That Convert

### Power Words (use in headlines and CTAs)
Free, New, Proven, Easy, Instant, Guaranteed, Save, Discover, Unlock, Transform, Boost, Eliminate, Simple, Fast, Powerful, Custom, Exclusive

### Words to Avoid (reduce conversions)
Maybe, Possibly, Try, Hope, Might, Some, Various, Several, Utilize, Leverage, Synergy, Solution (without context), Best-in-class, World-class, Revolutionary

### Readability Rules
- Sentences: 15-20 words max
- Paragraphs: 3-4 sentences max
- Syllables: prefer 1-2 syllable words
- Grade level: 6-8 Flesch-Kincaid
- Active voice: "We build" not "Schedules are built by"
- Specific numbers: "40%" not "significant improvement"
